Eleven Years of Added Time

November 14, 2026

The Comeback of Sentimental Arse

Arsenal won the league after two decades! I woke up at 1 a.m. in Bali, jet-lagged, and discovered what the result of the other match meant for Arsenal. I couldn't scream so I checked if my spouse was awake. She was as we were battling the same jet lag. In a hushed voice (kids were unsurprisingly sleeping deeply), I broke the news to her. Her response was, "How?"

Maybe this meant I would stop being stressed every weekend. I still get stressed, but now I am confident we'll find a way.

Anyway, eleven years have passed in a blink, since I last posted something here. So much has changed in between (I will come back to it later). I start recounting what happened in between, and eleven years feels about right.

The genesis

I don't think I have ever mentioned how this blog got its name. During the banter years, visiting my regular football forums felt like witnessing the end of days. I wanted to gauge the emotional temperature before diving in. Sentiment analysis supplied the first part of the name. The "Arse" part needs less explanation.

In the meantime

I got married, had two kids, lost a bunch of hair, and accidentally became a runner.

Then came AI, which made me rethink my whole purpose of existence. A little hyperbolic, perhaps, but whatever. For my original idea, that means I no longer need to train a model on a carefully labelled dataset to predict sentiment. I can just use Jev, the decision model. And it's cheap?! What a time to be alive!

The crisis

Back in those days, I had a lot of side projects which amounted to nothing, mostly because of the time they required. I still find it hard to build things. The problem has changed now: what do I want to build when I can build anything? What is worth building anymore? What would give me that sense of achievement?

Without a clear answer, I have decided to return to writing. It might be decent, or it might be mediocre, but it will still be mine. Something that I thought of and put into words. AI may help with the analysis. Once a week, once a fortnight - we'll see. For now, I'm back.
